### Where neighbouring tiers cost nearly the same but are shaped differently

Three pairs deserve a second look, because the price gap is small and the difference is not.

- **New Star vs Sirius.** $1.04/mo more doubles the disk from 10 GB to 20 GB and triples the port from 100 Mbps to 300 Mbps. For anything serving files or media, Sirius is the better buy.
- **Arcturus vs Double Star.** $6.92 against $7.08, effectively the same money. Double Star gives you a second core; Arcturus gives you 10 GB more disk and a faster port with only one core. Pick by what your workload runs out of first.
- **Alpha Centauri vs Double Star.** $1.91 more, same 2 cores and 2 GB, but double the disk (40 GB) and double the port (400 Mbps).
- **Bright Star vs Capella.** Both are 4 cores / 4 GB. Capella costs $8.50 more and the entire difference goes to storage (30 GB to 120 GB) and port speed (300 to 750 Mbps). If your bottleneck is CPU, neither one helps you.

## What the $1.99 does not cover

The pricing model is unusual and worth understanding before you compare it to anything else. In the site's own words: *"You only pay for disk space. Every plan includes unlimited traffic."* Bandwidth is not the meter; disk is. That is why a 10 Mbps port and a 512 MB slice can be sold for under two dollars.

Extra capacity is sold separately, and the add-on prices are where the budget quietly changes:

| Add-on | Price |
| --- | --- |
| IPv4 address | $3.31/month |
| +10 Mbps bandwidth | $2.50/month |
| Dedicated 1 Gbps bandwidth | $333.33/month |
| Sysadmin services | $9.16/hour |

Two of these numbers should stop you for a second. The +10 Mbps add-on costs **more than the Promo server itself**, so upgrading the port on the cheapest plan doubles the bill for a modest speed bump. And an hour of sysadmin time equals roughly four and a half months of the Promo plan, which tells you how the company expects managed work to be priced: as a separate service, not a bundled perk.

The "fair-share" label on every port figure matters too. Those speeds are shared ceilings, not a guaranteed pipe, and the fact that dedicated 1 Gbps is sold at $333.33/mo confirms the cheap tiers are sharing uplink with other customers. For a small site this is irrelevant. For anything with sustained throughput needs, budget for the dedicated option or pick a different provider.

## Three restrictions that only appear in the FAQ

None of these are on the pricing page, and all three affect what you actually get.

**No automatic backups.** The FAQ is blunt about it: *"No, we don't offer automatic backups. Instead, set up your own backup server and keep copies on a separate server for extra reliability."* You are responsible for your own snapshot routine. If you were assuming the host handles it, add a second cheap VPS or an object-storage bill to your budget before you order.

**IPv4 costs extra, IPv6 is free.** The site frames this as a feature: *"Free IPv6 address for VPS — Perfect for a budget virtual server: no need to buy an IPv4 address."* True if your users and your tooling are IPv6-ready. Most people still need IPv4 for mail, legacy clients and half the internet, so plan on $3.31/mo or check what you can live without.

**It is self-managed by default.** SSH access is included on every plan, and the assumption is that you can use it. Managed support exists but is billed hourly at $9.16. That is a reasonable arrangement for a developer and a poor one for someone who wants a control panel and a phone number.

## Who JustHost fits, and who should buy somewhere else

The company's own guidance is the most honest sizing advice available: a basic plan with minimal resources is enough for simple websites and small apps, while a high-traffic site, an online store or a database needs more RAM, CPU and disk. Translated into this lineup, that means Promo or New Star for a brochure site, Sirius or Arcturus for a small app with a database, and Vega upward once you are running a store or a busy backend.

Buy here if you are comfortable on the command line, want an inexpensive box in a specific country, and would rather pay $4.85 for a server than $4.85 for a control panel license. The free migration, free IPv6 and instant location switching in the client area all reduce the cost of getting it wrong.

Look elsewhere if you want cPanel shared hosting with a free domain and a published 30-day refund window, or Windows, or WordPress-specific managed hosting. justhost.asia publishes "easy cancellation" but no refund period, no trial and no money-back guarantee on its product pages. That is not a hidden trap, it is simply absent, and you should treat the absence as the answer.

One more piece of context for anyone worried about whether this is a real operation. Baxet Group's corporate blog describes JustHosting as the project the group grew out of, and justhost.ru lists BAXET LLC as its operating entity. justhost.asia's own pages don't name a legal entity, so the fair reading is one brand family with shared support language rather than a confirmed single company. The site reports 382,235 customers, 58,858 active VPS, 80 locations across 41 countries and 21 years in business, which is consistent with a mid-sized provider rather than a reseller front.

## Ordering, migrating and resizing

The purchase flow is five steps and takes minutes: choose a plan, click Order, complete the order form and payment, then you get access to the VPS control panel and settings once the payment clears. Nothing about it requires a sales call.

Migration is free and handled by their specialists, who move files, databases and server settings. The stated terms are no downtime and no data loss, and the only thing you have to supply is access to your current host. Set that up before you cancel the old server, not after.

Resizing is the part that removes most of the risk from picking a tier. CPU cores, RAM and disk can be added at any time without moving your data to a different machine, and the change applies with minimal downtime. So the sensible strategy is to start one tier below your estimate and grow into it, rather than overbuy on day one. The same applies to location: the client area lets you switch data center and change your IP address whenever you need to.
